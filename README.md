# Left Right

Unity 6.6 project (`6000.6.9f1`) containing the playable prototype and a separate starting point for its rebuild.

## Prototype snapshot

The annotated Git tag `prototype-v1` preserves the full working project before cleanup, including scripts, assets, package versions, settings, and smoke checks. A scene copy alone would still share those dependencies.

For an independent reference checkout, run `git worktree add --detach ../leftright-prototype prototype-v1` and open that directory as a separate Unity project. ## Rebuild

Open `Assets/Rebuild/Scenes/Main.unity`, or use **LeftRight > Open rebuild**. It contains a low-poly military outpost: four raised walkways, a command post, barracks, tents, sandbags, wire, watchtowers, and forest scenery. Put new scripts in `Assets/Rebuild/Scripts`, using the `LeftRight.Rebuild` namespace. Its assembly has no dependency on the prototype assembly. Main now has four controlled recruits and a beat-driven spike course, including elimination, game over and restart.

`RecruitController` owns input and movement, `RecruitMarch` owns the character animation, and `SpikeCourse` owns penalties and round results. The new course replaces platform shrinking with closing spike pillars. Player-count selection, menus and additional pickup types remain future work; the first health-crate powerup is connected.

Avoid editing prototype assets to serve the new game. Copy a needed visual asset into the rebuild through Unity so it gets its own GUID. Keep render pipeline settings shared until the rebuild needs different rendering. Audio is intentionally shared: `Assets/Rebuild/Settings/PrototypeAudio.asset` references the exact original title/round music, direction calls, footsteps, landing, and health-pickup clips. Play Mode waits three seconds, then starts the original title music on its opening beat; `OutpostAudio` exposes the other cues for the future game flow.

The prototype remains the enabled build scene. Main is listed but disabled; switch the build entry point when the rebuild is ready to ship. **LeftRight > Build prototype for Windows** always builds only the prototype.

## Spike pillars

Each walkway has five slim pillars directly on its five yellow end markers, at both ends: eight banks and forty pillars total. All pillars are fully raised from the start, including during the three-second silent lead-in and after restarting; there is no rise animation. The actual spike tips face inward. The platforms keep their full size.

On each master beat, standing still or moving opposite the officer advances both banks on that recruit's lane by 0.45 metres each. Correct movement leaves them still. The opening beat is free, and new cues give half a beat to react. The first spike contact pins that recruit upright: the tips penetrate the torso, movement and marching stop, and the recruit travels with that bank. The pinned lane keeps closing on missed beats. Contact from the opposite set completes elimination; the sets then finish closing until one walkway plank (0.25 metres) remains between their inner faces. The trapped recruit stays sideways and its model compresses along the closing direction to fit the gap. At full closure, a randomized low-poly blood burst splatters onto the walkway and spreads into a puddle. The flattened body releases into physics and falls onto the walkway; after a short hold, both spike banks slide back to the original lane ends. This sequence continues after game over; restart clears the liquid and restores the original proportions. Other lanes continue independently. When all four recruits are eliminated, music/cues stop and **Enter** restarts the course. Music pause also pauses recruit movement, penalties and pillar motion.

Only the local green recruit in the front lane reads this client's controls: **A/D**, or the current gamepad's left stick/D-pad. The other recruits ignore local hardware. `RecruitController.isLocallyControlled` separates input ownership from `playerIndex` (lane assignment); character selection/network identity should later map the local character into the front presentation slot. The full builder and recruit updater also install them. Editable art is `SourceArt/Environment/SpikePillar.blend`; regenerate it with `blender --background --python SourceArt/Environment/build_spike_pillar.py`. `Tools/Rebuild/VerifySpikeCourse.cs` passed 19 checks covering mesh orientation, full-height startup, penalties, correct/incorrect movement, pause, elimination, game over and restart. `VerifySpikePinning.cs` adds 18 checks for the final one-plank gap, mesh compression/restoration, both attachment sides, visible penetration depth, disabled input, carried travel, pause, second-set elimination, game over and reset. ## Health crate powerup

A small wooden medical crate with red crosses on pale panels restores room on the collecting recruit's lane. Collecting queues three outward recovery steps (1.35 metres per bank total at current tuning) and plays the prototype health-pickup sound. On each of the next three master beats, both banks step back one increment and hold there until the next beat. A recovery beat takes priority over an inward penalty; recovery clamps at the original lane width. Pinned/eliminated recruits cannot collect it.

`HealthCrateSpawner` lives under **Powerups**, separate from spike/obstacle logic. Each lane has independent, guaranteed spawn opportunities at beat 8 and every 16 beats thereafter. At least two missed beats of current lost space are required; full-width, pinned and eliminated lanes skip the opportunity. An existing crate also consumes/skips the slot. Collecting or expiring never resets the lane cooldown, and a seek does not replay missed slots. There is at most one crate per lane. Only placement is random: 1.5?2 metres to either side of the recruit, inside a margin of 0.8 metres from the spike tips. If neither side has enough safe space, that opportunity is skipped instead of clamping a crate underfoot. Crates bob gently on the master beat and expire after 12 beats or when advancing spikes reach them. Pausing music freezes its motion and lifetime; restart and game over clear all crates. Spawning uses its own random source so it does not change instructor cue timing. Spawn intervals, eligibility threshold, placement ranges, lifetime and restored beats have commented Inspector settings. The comments in `HealthCrateSpawner` explain the fairness, fixed schedule and safe-range decisions; pickup and trap comments explain one-shot collection, musical expiry and clamped recovery.

Use **LeftRight > Rebuild > Update health crate powerup** to install it independently; rebuilding the course/recruits also preserves it. The prefab is `Assets/Rebuild/Prefabs/HealthCrate.prefab`; editable art is `SourceArt/Environment/HealthCrate.blend`, regenerated with `blender --background --python SourceArt/Environment/build_health_crate.py`. `Tools/Rebuild/VerifyHealthCrate.cs` passed 17 checks for spawning, collection, recovery limits, lane ownership, sound, pause, expiry, pinned-player exclusion and reset.

`BloodSplash` uses low-poly droplets with gravity and swept collisions against scene colliders, a small energy-losing recoil, impact splats and a spreading irregular puddle. Droplets stretch with speed; off-edge droplets fall away. This is particle-based liquid presentation, not a full fluid solver. Each effect uses its own random seed without consuming gameplay randomness. `LaneSpikeTrap` triggers it only when the one-plank crush is complete, starts retracting immediately after the completed crush and returns in 1.2 seconds. The crushed recruit begins tipping slowly at the same time, randomly choosing left or right independently of which bank first hit it, and bends down as it falls. Its compressed proportions stay independent of the withdrawing banks. `VerifyBloodAndReturn.cs` passed 14 checks for full closure, droplets, platform impacts, pooling, variation, body release, bank return, game-over completion and restart cleanup.

First spike contact emits a smaller blood burst and puddle; full closure emits the larger squeeze splash. Both are owned by the lane and cleaned on reset. `RecruitRagdoll` snapshots the crushed skin and equipment once at release into a six-centimetre-thick flexible sheet. A small position-based cloth solver connects five strips with length and bending constraints; gravity, initial momentum and ground friction let the torso, head and legs flop with a two-second slow tip followed by a sharply accelerating drop. Once the upper body passes balance, the head and torso whip down in roughly 0.1 seconds; small solver substeps preserve the sheet and ground contact during this fast finish while preserving the silhouette. ## Local and Online camera modes

Camera behavior follows the session type, rather than a setting. Local games always show the full course; the close follow camera is reserved for future online matches. Starting a local game explicitly selects Local, and old camera preferences are ignored. Online Match is disabled in the start menu. The in-game gear opens the normal settings menu, which contains volume controls and no camera-mode selector. Victory sequences may still use their own temporary cinematic camera before returning to the full-course view.

Health recovery uses `LaneSpikeTrap.PendingRecoverySteps` instead of a multi-step sliding target. `ProcessRecoveryBeat` consumes at most one reserved step per musical beat; `SpikeCourse` gives that outward step priority over damage on the same beat. Recovery never begins on the pickup frame, pauses with the musical clock, and resets with the round. Eligibility uses the space remaining after reserved recovery so extra health cannot stack beyond the original lane width. `VerifyHealthBeatRecovery.cs` passed 14 actual-clock checks for three discrete steps, holds between them, penalty priority, pause and reset.

## Victory sequences

`SpikeCourse` ends a multiplayer round when exactly one recruit survives, checks the full contact batch before applying fatal hits and reserves one random winner if all final contenders would die together, and publishes `Completed(winner)`. Single-recruit test rounds continue normally. Gameplay input, penalties and officer cues stop at round end. Every dead lane finishes its crush and retracts; the winner's lane retracts too. Enter restarts as before.

`VictoryDirector` is separate from damage, movement and beat logic. Its Inspector field `victoryDelaySeconds` defaults to **1 second after the last opponent is eliminated**. A pending victory is cancelled by restart. The `sequences` array is an equal-chance pool of distinct, available `VictorySequence` assets: one gets 100%, two get 50% each, N get 1/N. Null entries, duplicates and assets marked unavailable are excluded. Selection happens immediately when the winner is decided, using independent cosmetic randomness with repeats allowed; only playback waits for the delay. An empty pool skips the celebration safely. Simultaneous final deaths use the same normal winner celebration.

The first asset is `Assets/Rebuild/Victories/TurnAndRaiseArms.asset`. It turns the survivor toward the camera, raises both arms into a victory V and smoothly moves/zooms the camera toward them over four seconds. Turn speed, arm timing/angle, zoom duration, distance and FOV are editable on the asset. Camera ownership passes temporarily from `GameViewSettings` to the sequence in both view modes and returns on cancellation/restart.

To add the next 20–30 celebrations, derive a new ScriptableObject from `VictorySequence`, implement `CreatePlayback(winner, camera)` and keep mutable per-run state in the returned `VictoryPlayback`. `Tick(elapsedSeconds)` drives the pose/camera and `Stop()` releases/restores any resources owned by that playback. Create an asset and add it to `VictoryDirector.sequences`; round logic and selection need no changes. Shared assets are configuration only, so separate runs never reuse animation state. **LeftRight > Rebuild > Update victory system** installs the director and first asset without rebuilding the scene; the course builder includes it too.

`VerifyVictory.cs` passed 23 checks for the survivor, delay, pose, zoom, spike return, pool filtering/random selection, empty pool, tied final deaths and reset/cancellation. `VerifyVictoryCharacters.cs` passed 16 checks covering all four winner rigs and camera framing.
Victory playback is capped by `VictoryDirector.victoryDurationSeconds` (default **5 seconds**), followed by a smooth **1.5-second** return to the saved full-course camera (`zoomOutSeconds`). The selected sequence holds its final pose during the pullback, then releases its resources. Online follow stays suspended in the round summary so it cannot zoom back in behind the **Start again** prompt. The button calls `StartAgain()`; Enter still restarts. Restart/cancellation clears the prompt, camera ownership and cheer in every phase.

A dedicated non-looping 2D AudioSource plays `Audio/Victory/Cheers.ogg` exactly when celebration playback starts, respecting the existing `AudioLevel` preference. It is separate from gameplay audio, so ending the round does not silence it. The recording is [Cheers by AuraVoice / Nocturnal_Vanguard](https://opengameart.org/content/cheers-0), CC0; provenance is recorded beside the clip. `cheerClip`, `cheerVolume`, duration and pullback timing are editable on the director.

The 16 checks in `VerifyVictoryFinish.cs` cover cheer data and playback, the five-second limit, smooth pullback, stable wide framing, prompt timing and restarting during celebration. The existing 23 victory checks also pass.


Winner protection is immediate: the course predicts fatal contacts across every lane before applying them. If only one safe recruit remains, that recruit is reserved; if all remaining contenders would die in that frame, one is chosen uniformly at random. Earlier casualties never enter this draw.
